B-Tree and B+ Tree

Understand balanced multiway search trees used in database indexes.

1. Why do databases use these trees?

Databases can store millions of records. A binary search tree may be tall, causing many disk reads. B-Trees and B+ Trees have many children per node, stay balanced, and reduce the number of levels that must be visited.

Balanced

Leaves are kept at the same depth (in standard B/B+ tree definitions).

High fan-out

A node can contain multiple keys and child pointers, reducing tree height.

Disk-friendly

Node sizes can be designed to match database pages or blocks.

2. Compare B-Tree and B+ Tree

B-Tree

Keys and record pointers may appear in internal nodes as well as leaves. A search can sometimes finish before reaching a leaf.

B+ Tree

Internal nodes guide searches; actual data entries/record pointers are stored at the leaves. Leaf nodes are commonly linked for efficient range scans.

FeatureB-TreeB+ Tree
Where records/data pointers liveMay appear in internal nodes and leavesAt leaf level
Internal nodesKeys plus child pointers; may also store record pointersSeparator keys and child pointers
Range queriesPossible, but leaf traversal is less uniformEfficient with linked leaves
Internal fan-outOften lower if nodes store record pointersOften higher because internal nodes store only routing information

Exact node-capacity rules depend on the textbook's definition of tree order and implementation details.

4. Interactive insertion idea

Inserting a key into a full node can cause a split. A separator key is promoted to the parent; if the parent is also full, splitting may propagate upward. This keeps the tree balanced.

Exam recap

  • B-Trees and B+ Trees are balanced multiway search trees.
  • High fan-out means fewer levels and potentially fewer disk I/Os.
  • In a typical B-Tree, record pointers may be stored in internal nodes and leaves.
  • In a typical B+ Tree, data entries/record pointers are at the leaves; internal nodes direct the search.
  • Linked B+ Tree leaves make sequential and range access efficient.
  • Insertions and deletions may trigger splits, merges, or redistribution to preserve constraints.

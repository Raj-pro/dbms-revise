Relationship-Set Constraints

Learn cardinality ratios, participation constraints, and how to read them in an ER model.

1. The core idea

An entity is a thing (such as Student or Course). A relationship connects entities (such as Student enrolls in Course). Relationship-set constraints tell us how many entities can be connected and whether participation is compulsory.

Cardinality ratio

How many entities on one side can be associated with an entity on the other side?

Participation constraint

Must every entity take part in the relationship, or is participation optional?

5. Remember the difference

  • Cardinality: how many can be associated? (1:1, 1:N, N:1, M:N)
  • Participation: must every entity participate? (total or partial)
  • These are independent ideas. A relationship can be 1:N and still have partial participation on one or both sides.
  • In a relational database, a 1:N relationship is commonly implemented by placing a foreign key on the N-side. An M:N relationship is commonly implemented with a separate junction table.

Conflict Serializability

Find conflicting operations, build a precedence graph, and determine whether a schedule is conflict-serializable.

1. When do two operations conflict?

Two operations conflict if they belong to different transactions, access the same data item, and at least one of them is a write.

Read–Write (RW)

R1(X) before W2(X) — conflict.

Write–Read (WR)

W1(X) before R2(X) — conflict.

Write–Write (WW)

W1(X) before W2(X) — conflict.

Read–Read (RR)

R1(X) and R2(X) — no conflict.

3. Rule to decide serializability

Acyclic graph

If the precedence graph has no directed cycle, the schedule is conflict-serializable.

Cyclic graph

If the graph contains a directed cycle, the schedule is not conflict-serializable.

Equivalent serial order

For an acyclic graph, a topological ordering of the transactions gives a conflict-equivalent serial schedule.

Exam recap

  1. Find pairs of operations from different transactions on the same data item where at least one is a write.
  2. For each conflict ordered Ti before Tj, add edge Ti → Tj.
  3. Check the precedence graph for cycles.
  4. No cycle → conflict-serializable; cycle → not conflict-serializable.

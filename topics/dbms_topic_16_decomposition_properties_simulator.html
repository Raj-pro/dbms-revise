Properties of Decomposition

Understand lossless join and dependency preservation with tables and interactive checks.

1. Why decompose a relation?

Normalization often splits a large table into smaller tables. A good decomposition should avoid losing information and should make it possible to enforce the original functional dependencies conveniently.

2. Lossless join

A decomposition is lossless if joining the smaller relations reconstructs exactly the original relation—no missing tuples and no extra spurious tuples.

Original table = Join of decomposed tables
Binary decomposition rule: For R split into R₁ and R₂, the decomposition is lossless with respect to F if the common attributes determine all attributes of at least one side. Formally, (R₁ ∩ R₂) → R₁ or (R₁ ∩ R₂) → R₂ must follow from F.

3. Dependency preservation

A decomposition is dependency-preserving if the original functional dependencies can be checked on the smaller relations individually, without joining them back together.

Check local tables → enforce original FDs
If an original dependency needs a join across tables to verify it, that dependency may not be preserved locally.

7. Remember

  • Lossless join: protects information when tables are joined back.
  • Dependency preservation: protects easy enforcement of functional dependencies.
  • They are different properties; a decomposition may satisfy one without satisfying the other.

Non-Prime Attribute — Interactive Simulator

Learn how to identify attributes that do not belong to any candidate key.

1. What is a non-prime attribute?

A non-prime attribute is an attribute that is not part of any candidate key for that relation.

Prime attribute

Appears in at least one candidate key.

Example: StudentID

Non-prime attribute

Appears in no candidate key.

Example: Grade (in our example)

Memory trick: Prime = part of a candidate key. Non-prime = not part of any candidate key.

2. Classify the attributes

Consider ENROLLMENT(StudentID, CourseID, Grade), where the only candidate key is (StudentID, CourseID).

AttributeCandidate key membershipClassification
StudentIDPart of (StudentID, CourseID)Prime
CourseIDPart of (StudentID, CourseID)Prime
GradeNot part of any candidate keyNon-prime
All attributes are visible. Grade is the non-prime attribute in this example.

4. Why it matters in normalization

Prime and non-prime attributes help us understand normal forms, especially Second Normal Form (2NF) and Third Normal Form (3NF).

Preview: In 2NF, we check whether a non-prime attribute depends on only part of a composite candidate key. In 3NF, prime attributes also matter when checking functional dependencies.

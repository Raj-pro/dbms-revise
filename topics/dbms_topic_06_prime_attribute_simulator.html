Prime Attribute — Interactive Simulator

Find which columns belong to at least one candidate key.

1. What is a prime attribute?

A prime attribute is an attribute that is part of at least one candidate key. A non-prime attribute is not part of any candidate key.

Prime attribute

Belongs to one or more candidate keys.

Memory clue: “part of a key”

Non-prime attribute

Does not belong to any candidate key.

Memory clue: “not part of a key”

Important: Prime does not mean “primary key.” A prime attribute can belong to a candidate key that was not selected as the primary key.

2. See it in a relation

Consider ENROLLMENT(StudentID, CourseID, Grade), with candidate key (StudentID, CourseID).

AttributePart of a candidate key?Classification
StudentIDYes — part of (StudentID, CourseID)Prime ✓
CourseIDYes — part of (StudentID, CourseID)Prime ✓
GradeNoNon-prime
StudentID and CourseID form the candidate key, so both are prime. Grade is not part of any candidate key in this example.

3. Change the candidate keys

Choose a scenario and watch how prime/non-prime classification changes.

AttributeCandidate key(s) in scenarioClassification
StudentID(StudentID, CourseID)Prime
CourseID(StudentID, CourseID)Prime
GradeNoneNon-prime
Scenario A: the composite candidate key includes StudentID and CourseID, so both are prime.

Scenario B is a hypothetical schema where StudentID alone uniquely identifies a row. In that case, CourseID and Grade are non-prime unless they belong to another candidate key.

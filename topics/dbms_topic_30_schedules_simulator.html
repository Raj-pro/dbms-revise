Transaction Schedules

Learn serial and concurrent schedules, operation ordering, and why interleaving matters.

1. What is a schedule?

A schedule is an ordering of operations from one or more transactions, while preserving the order of operations within each individual transaction.

Serial schedule

One transaction completes before the next begins. Simple to reason about, but it may reduce concurrency.

Non-serial schedule

Operations from multiple transactions are interleaved. It can improve concurrency, but correctness must be checked.

Notation: R1(X) = transaction T1 reads X; W2(X) = T2 writes X; C1 = T1 commits; A2 = T2 aborts.

3. Serial vs. non-serial

PropertySerialNon-serial
InterleavingNoYes
ConcurrencyLimitedPotentially higher
Correctness guarantee by itselfSerial execution is a baseline for comparisonMust analyze whether it is equivalent to a correct serial execution, plus recovery concerns

A non-serial schedule is not automatically incorrect. Many interleaved schedules are valid; the goal is to allow concurrency without violating correctness.

Exam recap

  • A schedule orders reads, writes, commits, and aborts from transactions.
  • A serial schedule has no interleaving between transactions.
  • A non-serial schedule interleaves operations and may permit more concurrency.
  • Each transaction's internal operation order must be preserved.
  • Conflict serializability and view serializability are separate tests covered next in the original syllabus.
